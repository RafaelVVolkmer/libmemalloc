# Structure reference

These structures create predictable cadence and hide missing evidence. Rewrite
the underlying claim. A punctuation change preserves the same template.

## Formulaic contrast

Avoid templates such as `not X, but Y` and `it is not X, it is Y`. State the
positive claim and supply its evidence.

## Negative list

Avoid a sequence such as `not A, not B, and not C`. Name the property that
excludes those cases.

## Dramatic fragment

Avoid isolated lines such as `Full stop`, `Read that again`, or
`Let that sink in`. Keep the evidence in the paragraph that interprets it.

## Rhetorical setup

Rewrite sentences that begin with a Wh-word as factual statements. Reserve a
question for procedures that require an answer from the reader.

## False agency

Name the human actor. Prefer `We concluded from the measurements` over
`The study concluded`. Describe machine behavior with an operation and output.

## Meta-joiner

Delete navigation prose such as `The next section explains` or
`We will now discuss`. A descriptive heading supplies enough navigation.

## Mechanical rhythm

Read each paragraph aloud and mark sentence length, opening form, and ending.
Revise repeated patterns. Use two examples if the third adds no evidence.
Integrate a short conclusion into its supporting paragraph if it resembles a
slogan or pull-quote.

## Punctuation

Use periods, commas, colons, or parentheses in place of em dashes. Choose the
mark that represents the grammatical relationship.
